Citing SALT
===========

When using the inflow model, cite:

* Carr, C. & Scarlata, C. (2022), *A Semianalytical Line Transfer Model. III.
  Galactic Inflows*, ApJ, 939, 47, `doi:10.3847/1538-4357/ac93fa
  <https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/ac93fa>`_.

When using the turbulent outflow model, cite:

* Carr et al. (2023), *Testing SALT Approximations with Numerical Radiation
  Transfer Code. I. Validity and Applicability*, ApJ, 952, 88,
  `doi:10.3847/1538-4357/acd331
  <https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/acd331>`_.
* Carr et al. (2026), *Testing SALT Approximations with Numerical Radiative
  Transfer Code. II. Thermal and Microturbulent Line Broadening*, manuscript
  associated with this software release.

Physical foundation
-------------------

The broader SALT framework was introduced and extended in:

* Scuderi, S., Bonanno, G., Di Benedetto, R., Spadaro, D. & Panagia, N.
  (1992), *H-alpha Observations of Early-Type Stars*, ApJ, 392, 201--208.
* Scarlata & Panagia (2015), *A Semi-Analytical Line Transfer (SALT) Model to
  Interpret the Spectra of Galaxy Outflows*, ApJ, 801, 43.
* Carr et al. (2018), *A Semi-analytical Line Transfer (SALT) Model. II. The
  Effects of a Bi-conical Geometry*, ApJ, 860, 143.
* Carr, Scarlata, Henry & Panagia (2021), *The Effect of Bi-conical Outflows
  on Ly\ :math:`\alpha` Escape from Green Peas*, ApJ, 906, 104,
  `doi:10.3847/1538-4357/abc7c3
  <https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/abc7c3>`_.
